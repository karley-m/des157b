Bibliography

“Wildland Urban Interface [WUI] Research for Resilience – Center for Critical Urban and Environmental Studies.” Ucsc.edu, 2024, criticalurbanenvironments.ucsc.edu/projects/wildland-urban-interface-wui-research-for-resilience/
The Center for Critical Urban and Environmental Studies at UC Santa Cruz is a project that studies how California’s housing crisis is connected to climate risk, wildfire exposure, and habitat loss. WUI which stands for Wildland-Urban Interface is something we are seeing more and more people push into as housing costs in cities rise and these regions are vulnerable to biodiversity loss, flooding, and wildfires. This project mentions that solving the housing crisis, while it may be a complex issue, is also a climate adaptation strategy. Housing affordability is a major driver of WUI growth, and these regions are increasing commute times.

“Greenpeace International.” Greenpeace International, 2 Apr. 2026, www.greenpeace.org/international/story/82398/five-reasons-safe-sustainable-affordable-housing-cost-of-living-climate/.
Greenpeace launched an initiative aimed at drawing attention to the importance of safe sustainable living. Emphasizing a home is more than a roof over our heads, it's our community. They need to be part of the solution to the climate emergency when poor energy efficiency and fossil fuel dependence worsen energy security, and therefore the climate crisis. Relevant to my project ending on a hopeful note, that a switch to clean energy and a reimagination of housing is accessible.

“False Alarm — Real Life.” Real Life, 2019, reallifemag.com/false-alarm/.‌
Smart home surveillance technologies like Ring doorbell cameras reshape people’s perceptions of safety, crime, and community. She argues these systems produce anxiety and suspicion. Suburban neighborhoods have normalized Ring systems and similar devices. Capitalism has turned fear into a profitable business model. This intensifies suburban isolation in some facets. The boundary between private home security and broader social policing is blurred. I thought this was an interesting read and could factor into the kinds of relationships to communities we may see in the future.

“Ghosts of the Future — Real Life.” Real Life, 2019, reallifemag.com/ghosts-of-the-future/.
This author explored how smart-home technologies resemble haunted houses of horror fiction and argues that devices like Amazon Alexa, Google Home, smart lights, automated surveillance systems, etc. behave in ways that feel uncanny/ghostlike. They further argue that the source of horror isn’t the machines itself but the human systems behind them which make the “smart-home” feel effortless, but conceal the labor required to sustain them. Within broader discussions of surveillance and automation, these devices that collect data on domestic life make the home less private. This promise of comfort and convenience from smart-home devices comes with increased dependence on technology that people can’t fully control or understand.
