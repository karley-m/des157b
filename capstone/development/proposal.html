Capstone Proposal

The topic I want to explore for my capstone project is: what will housing need to look like in the future as energy infrastructure changes and climate change intensifies?

Housing needs are typically met through the same developer-driven models (primarily in North America): single-unit family suburban housing or expensive townhouses. These houses are cheaply built, designed for speed of construction and profit. They do not take into consideration their environment, and are replicated all over the place. They use a lot of energy, make us car-dependent, and even socially isolated.

Between 1908 and 1940, Sears, the clothing store, used to sell prefabricated home kits through catalogs. These homes democratized access to housing and introduced a modular, participatory model of construction, like an extreme version of IKEA. Many of these homes still exist today showing their durability. While something like this couldn’t work today, could it work tomorrow (the future)?

This project will brief the user with some context on this possible future scenario where we are met with mass migrations, a constant new housing need, and climate resiliency while we reverse our destructive ways. After being briefed, I will allude to the Sears home kit as a basis of inspiration. Each step, the user will be met with an ability to choose what their house looks like, what kind of energy it uses, what it's made out of, water systems, food systems, and social systems. By hovering over each option in each section, the user will be able to view more information on this option including its sustainability level, efficiency, independence, lifecycle, cost curve over time. After each selection, the model will be added to the user’s “basket.” At the end of the journey, the basket will be emptied and the user will be able to drag and drop the images and illustrations to assemble their house. From there, they will be able to save the image and/or upload it to the “neighborhood,” the last section which reveals every participant’s house and what it looks like. This is to represent interconnectedness and collective adaptation. Since this is a speculative design project developed through research of alternative housing, it will not attempt to fully solve issues like land ownership, zoning laws, construction regulation, or development rights. Instead, this project functions as a thought experiment.

Possible challenges I’m thinking of right now is how to capture the user's creation at the end and display it on a separate page that includes all user inputs. I experimented with something like this for my Library Liberty project, however it was a text string instead of an image.

My main target audience for this project would be young adults to middle age adults who may be thinking about housing in their future.
